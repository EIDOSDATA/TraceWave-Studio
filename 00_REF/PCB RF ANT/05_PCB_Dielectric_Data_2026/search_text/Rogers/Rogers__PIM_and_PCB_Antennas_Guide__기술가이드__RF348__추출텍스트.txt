Passive Intermodulation (PIM) has been a concern in wireless
transmission systems since the 1970s. PIM affects systems
that use a single antenna for both transmitting and receiving
signals on multiple carrier frequencies. Slight non-linearities
in the input/output relationship in the system can generate
additional signals that are in-band at very low power levels
that can increase the noise-floor. Increased noise degrades
system performance. Its effects are becoming more notice-

Both families of circuit materials contribute very little to an antenna’s PIM levels. But, by applying
lessons learned from the extensive testing and research, Rogers’ has managed to exceed
even this low level of PIM, with its RO4730G3 antenna-grade circuit materials.
These thermoset laminates feature Dk values of 2.55 and 3.0, respectively, with
loss tangents of 0.0028 and 0.0029, respectively. For antenna designers
(or designers of passive components concerned with PIM), the PIM levels
 when measured with the standard test circuit and measurement setup
were exceedingly low: <-160 dBc, both for measurements on 0.030-in.
(1.5 mm) thick laminates.

These three materials exhibit almost unmeasurable PIM levels and,
along with the RO4500 and IM Series laminates, provide excellent
foundations for antennas and other passive circuits where PIM must
be minimized. PIM can result from many factors in a circuit or system,
and even the thickness and dielectric constant of a laminate can affect
PIM by contributing to the physical dimensions of transmission lines
leading to higher current densities in densely spaced circuits. However,
with these circuit laminates as starting points, antenna designers can be
assured of having an opportunity to create an antenna design with the
lowest PIM possible.
